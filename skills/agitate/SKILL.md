# agitate

The additive counterpart to `deslop` and `compress`. Those are subtractive — they produce a *clean* draft, which can still be mechanical: a page that passes every gate and still reads as a document, not a person. Subtraction can't revive a mechanical draft. Only addition can, and the material has to come from the author, because the one thing a model cannot invent is what actually happened to you.

## When to use
- "agitate this" / "make it more alive" / "reads like a document, not a person"
- "it's too clean but dead" / "it passes every check and still feels flat"
- In two moments: **before** drafting long-form (let the real spine emerge, then lock it), and as a
  **respin** when a gate-clean draft still feels dead.

## The core move: mine, then interview
An additive pass cannot supply the lived specific out of thin air — if it tries, it invents, and
invention is just higher-grade slop. So agitate works in two beats:

1. **Mine** the draft for where it goes generic — the places it *gestures* at something specific
   ("teams often struggle with this") instead of naming it, the abstractions standing in for a
   scene, the smooth transitions hiding a real tension.
2. **Interview** the author at each of those points. Ask for the concrete thing the draft is
   avoiding: the actual number, the specific failure, the person who said the thing, the moment it
   went wrong, the opinion the author actually holds and has been hedging. Then let the answer
   replace the abstraction.

The draft cannot answer these questions; only the author can. agitate's job is to ask the right
ones at the right places.

## The three agitations

1. **Collide the registers.** A dead draft usually stays at one elevation the whole way. Find where
   a different register belongs — a blunt short line after a long reasoned passage, a plain admission
   inside a formal argument — and let the voices collide instead of sealing each in its own paragraph.
2. **Find where it should spill.** Look for the idea that is being kept tidy when it wants to run
   over its banks — the point the author clearly cares about but has boxed into one careful sentence.
   Let it spill: give it the room and the messiness it's asking for.
3. **Surface the scar.** The lived, specific, slightly-costly detail is what a subtractive pass can
   never keep, because it was never in the draft for the gate to save. Interview for it and put it in.

## Workflow
1. **Read the draft for deadness**, not for errors — a gate already found the errors. Ask: where does
   this stop sounding like a person who was actually there?
2. **Mark the agitation points** — every place the draft goes generic, tidy, or single-register.
3. **Interview the author** at each point with a specific question. Do NOT answer for them.
4. **Fold the answers in**, colliding registers and letting the cared-about idea spill.
5. **Return** the marked-up draft plus the questions and the author's answers, so the revival is
   traceable and the author stays the source of every real detail.

## Where it sits
- Runs AFTER the subtractive gates (`deslop`, `compress`) — you agitate what survives, not raw slop.
- Complements `voice-judge`: the judge tells you a sentence is generic; agitate supplies the specific
  thing that makes it yours.

## Honest limit
agitate cannot invent your life. If the author has no real specific to give at an agitation point,
the honest move is to cut the claim, not manufacture a scar. A fabricated lived detail is the worst
slop of all, because it reads as authentic and isn't.
