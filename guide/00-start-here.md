# Start Here

This is a field guide and a small toolchain for one problem: keeping AI-assisted writing from reading
as machine-made. It has two layers. The **utility layer** is the tooling you run — rules files your
editor or agent can load, plus skills that draft, check, and compress a piece against the standard. The
**authority layer** is `guide/`: the essays and principles that explain *why* the tooling cuts what it
cuts, so you can make the calls the tooling can't. The tooling catches the obvious tells fast. The guide
is for the judgment behind them, because a clean draft and a good draft are not the same thing.

## The 60-second quickstart: Strip, Restore, Verify

You have a draft an AI helped write. Before it goes anywhere, run three passes.

**Strip** — cut the machine tells.
- Hedges and throat-clearing: "It's worth noting that," "It's important to remember," "Interestingly."
  Delete them and start on the substance.
- Generic transitions: "Let's dive in," "In today's landscape," "When it comes to," "In conclusion."
  Delete, or replace with the actual point.
- Structure-serving filler: pre-announcements ("In this piece we'll cover"), summary paragraphs that
  restate what you just said, timeline ramps ("First, then, finally") used as padding.

**Restore** — put a person back in.
- Vary the sentence lengths on purpose. A short line after a long one carries weight.
- Take a stance. Swap "some argue" for what you actually think, and keep the honest hedge where you're
  genuinely unsure ("I'm not certain about X, but").
- Trade abstraction for specifics. "Many companies" becomes a number or a named example. "A big
  improvement" becomes the actual figure.

**Verify** — read it against real practice.
- Cut anything that only serves the article's structure.
- Keep anything that proves you were actually there: the specific number, the real tool name, the
  changed-my-mind admission, the thing that cost you something to learn.

If a pass leaves the draft cleaner but still lifeless, that is the signal to go up a level into the
guide. Subtraction gets you to clean. It does not get you to a voice.

## Map of the guide

- **00 start-here** — this page.
- **01 slop-taxonomy** — the named tells, so you can point at what's wrong instead of just feeling it.
- **02 you-cant-subtract-to-a-voice** — why removing slop reaches clean-generic, not you. The core claim.
- **03 stance-vs-cadence** — the two things a reader actually hears: what you believe, and the rhythm
  you say it in.
- **04 three-altitudes** — moving between the concrete, the tactical, and the reflective inside one
  piece, instead of holding a single elevation the whole way.
- **05 the-chain** — the draft, check, edit, compress sequence, and where a human decision belongs in it.
- **06 build-a-voice-corpus** — collecting your own real passages so a draft imitates you, not the
  generic mean.
- **07 verify-the-verifier** — why a check that cannot fail when the work is wrong is not a check.
- **08 enforcement-honesty** — what the tooling actually catches, what it misses, and where it is a
  tripwire rather than a wall.
- **principles/** — the working reference: the content principles and the productive tensions that hold
  them in balance.
