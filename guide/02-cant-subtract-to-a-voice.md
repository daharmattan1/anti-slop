# You can't subtract your way to a voice

A slop filter knows exactly where the slop is. It has no idea where you are.

A filter like that does its job. Give it a list of banned phrases, a cap on em-dashes, a scanner for the tired constructions you never want to read again, and the junk you can name stops showing up. Then you read one of the clean drafts out loud, and the trouble is that nothing about it is yours. It is competent, it is correct, and it could have come from anyone.

Here is the geometry that finally made sense of it. Picture the whole space of sentences you could write. Slop is not spread evenly across it; it clumps in a few recognizable directions, the filler openers, the over-balanced clauses, the manufactured profundity, the endings engineered to sound quotable. A subtractive gate is a set of walls that shove you out of those clumps. Shove hard enough and you arrive at the dead center: no slop, and no signal either, the prose anyone produces once they have successfully avoided every mistake. Your actual voice is a specific point sitting off in some direction of its own, and nothing in a list of banned phrases points toward it. The list was assembled out of slop, so slop is the only thing it can ever find.

The filter can only take you to clean-generic, the tidy midpoint of careful writing that everyone converges on and no one actually lives at. Getting from there to a voice runs the other way, by addition.

There are three things worth adding, and this collection takes each one in turn. The first is to draft from real points of your own instead of from the crowd. Asking a model to write in your voice just runs the request back through its average of everyone who writes a little like you, which is the generic center again by another road. Hand it two or three real passages you actually wrote and tell it to keep going, and the output stops being an impression of you and starts being derived from you. (Building that set of passages is its own job; the corpus essay covers it.) The second addition is a judge that reads a draft next to your real samples and, for every sentence that did not come from the same hand, points you back at the closest real one. The third is to rough the draft up on purpose, colliding the registers you actually write in and digging up the specific lived detail a clean draft only gestures at. The altitudes essay and the stance essay work those two through.

None of the three is available to a subtractive pass, because none of them is about removal. You cannot cut your way to a claim you did not make.

I still run the checklist every time. It is cheap, and it kills the obvious junk in a second. What I stopped doing is reading a passing score as evidence the writing is any good. Clean is the floor, not the top of it, and everything that makes a page sound like a person happens after the gate is done.
