# Before / after — the taxonomy in action

Five synthetic pairs, each demonstrating a specific category from `guide/01-slop-taxonomy.md`.
The "before" text is deliberately slopped; the "after" is what survives the chain. These are
invented for teaching — swap in your own once you see the moves.

---

## 1. Filler openers + abstract framing (Category 1)

**Before**
> In today's rapidly evolving business landscape, it's worth noting that customer retention
> plays a pivotal role in the realm of sustainable growth.

**After**
> Keeping customers is cheaper than finding new ones, and most companies act like they don't
> know it.

*What changed:* cut two filler openers ("In today's… landscape", "it's worth noting that") and
two abstract-framing phrases ("plays a pivotal role", "in the realm of"). The after makes an
actual claim you could disagree with.

---

## 2. The shared dramatic-pause budget + staccato closer (Category 2)

**Before**
> The strategy was simple — brutally simple. Ship fast. Learn faster. And that changes
> everything.

**After**
> The strategy was simple: ship something small every week and let what happened teach us what
> to do next.

*What changed:* the before spends its whole dramatic-pause budget in one breath (an em-dash, two
fragments, and an aphoristic "and that changes everything" closer). The after states the actual
strategy instead of performing it.

---

## 3. Fabricated precision + vague attribution (Category 4)

**Before**
> Studies show that 87% of high-performing teams leverage AI to unlock exponential productivity
> gains.

**After**
> Teams that adopted the tool early told us it saved them a few hours a week on the reporting
> nobody wanted to do. We didn't measure "productivity"; we asked what got easier.

*What changed:* dropped the sourceless "87%" (fabricated precision), the "studies show" (vague
attribution), and the enthusiasm words ("leverage", "unlock", "exponential"). The after says what
was actually observed and admits what wasn't measured.

---

## 4. Guru + hustle voice (Category 3)

**Before**
> Here's the thing nobody tells you: to truly move the needle, you have to master the art of
> saying no and double down on your north star.

**After**
> The hard part isn't picking priorities. It's telling the three people whose projects you cut
> that you cut them, and meaning it.

*What changed:* removed the guru setup ("here's the thing nobody tells you"), the magic adverb
("truly"), and the hustle stack ("move the needle", "master the art of", "double down", "north
star"). The after names the specific uncomfortable thing the platitude was avoiding.

---

## 5. Clean-generic vs. a stance (the tell a scanner can't see)

Both of these pass every banned-phrase check. Only one has a point of view.

**Before (clean, but no one home)**
> There are several factors to consider when choosing a database, and the right choice depends
> on your specific needs and use case.

**After (same topic, with a stance)**
> Most teams reach for Postgres and are done thinking about it, which is usually correct. Reach
> for anything else only when you can name the specific thing Postgres won't do for you.

*What changed:* nothing a regex would flag — the before has no banned phrases. What it lacks is a
claim, a preference, and a cost. The after takes a position you could argue with. See
`guide/03-stance-vs-cadence.md`.
