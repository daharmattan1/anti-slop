# Anti-Slop Writing Rules

Drop this into your `CLAUDE.md` (or a file it imports) to hold AI-assisted writing to a
standard. These rules make writing read less like a machine wrote it. They are a *floor* —
they remove known tells; they do not supply a voice. Full reasoning: the `anti-slop` guide.

## The one question, first
Before editing anything, ask: **does this say something specific, or does it just have the
shape of insight?** If it sounds authoritative but a reader couldn't act on it, no line-edit
saves it — rewrite it. A draft that could be mass-produced with the topic swapped out is slop
no matter how clean the sentences are.

## Category 1 — Banned phrases (zero tolerance)
Never use these. Delete or rewrite on sight.
- **Filler openers:** "It's worth noting that", "It's important to note", "In today's
  landscape", "As we all know", "In essence", "At its core", "When it comes to".
- **Abstract framing:** "the realm of", "navigate the complexities of", "a testament to",
  "plays a pivotal role", "underscores", "lies at the heart of".
- **Generic enthusiasm:** "game-changer", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "unlock",
  "supercharge", "seamless", "robust", "powerful" (as filler).
- **Formal transitions:** "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally", "That being said".
- **Magic adverbs (hollow intensifiers):** "quietly", "simply", "truly", "genuinely",
  "effectively", "significantly", "seamlessly" — cut unless they carry real information.
- **Avoidance verbs:** "serves as", "acts as", "functions as" — usually a dodge for "is".
- **Vague attributions:** "studies show", "experts say", "research suggests" — name the
  source or cut the claim.

## Category 2 — Structure & cadence
- **Shared dramatic-pause budget (the key rule):** em-dashes, colon-sentences ("The truth:
  X"), and dramatic sentence fragments all make the same pause-then-deliver beat, so they draw
  from ONE budget — about one dramatic pause of any kind per 150 words, max. Counting only
  em-dashes misses it.
- **No aphoristic / staccato closers as a habit.** The two-beat mic-drop ("X. Not Y." / "A
  person does.") is allowed at most once in a whole piece, never section after section. Read
  each section's LAST sentence in isolation; if they all land a quotable turn, flatten them.
- **No tricolon abuse** (rule-of-three rhythm used as decoration more than once or twice).
- **Vary sentence length.** Avoid a run of near-identical-length sentences.
- **No summary paragraph** that restates instead of advancing.

## Category 3 — Voice
- No guru language ("the secret to", "here's the thing", "let that sink in").
- No hustle / consulting-speak ("leverage", "synergy", "move the needle", "at the end of the day").
- No passive authority: no sweeping claim without a citation or a stated cost.
- No invented concept labels — don't Capitalize an ordinary idea into a Framework it hasn't earned.

## Category 4 — Content sins
- **No fabricated precision.** No oddly specific numbers ("73% of teams") without a real source.
- **No stat recycling** — don't repeat one figure as if it were several proofs.
- **No signposted conclusions** ("In conclusion", "The bottom line is").
- **No one-point dilution** — don't pad one idea to hit a length.
- **Retire a metaphor after one or two uses;** never farm the same image 4+ times.

## Category 5 — Outbound / sales copy (if applicable)
- No empty personalization ("I came across your profile").
- Lead with the reader's problem, not "About us".
- Benefits over feature lists; one clear ask, not a premature "grab 30 minutes?".

## Category 6 — Conversion copy (if applicable)
- Specific over generic; every claim concrete enough to picture.
- One clear CTA; benefit-led, not vendor-led language.

## Category 7 — AI composition tropes
- No invented frameworks, no false vulnerability ("I'll be honest…" as a move), no magic
  adverbs, no "serves as" dodges. These are the same tells as above, concentrated.

## The honest limit
Following these gets you to *clean*, not to *good*. Clean is a page with nothing wrong on it;
whether there's anything worth reading is a separate question these rules can't answer. After
the draft is clean, do the additive work: write toward real samples of a specific voice, and
put in the concrete, lived specifics no rule can supply.
